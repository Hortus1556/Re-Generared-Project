# Amateur Surgeon Christmas, rebuilt from the Flash original

Christmas is rebuilt on the engine from the original Flash game (Adult Swim /
Mediatonic, 2008), preserved by Flashpoint in `re/flash/xmas/`. It is an
extension of AS1 ([as1.md](as1.md)): the same Flash surgeon game, set up for
Christmas's package, run the same way and with the same fixes.

- **Art and sounds.** The SWF's art and sounds are the game's assets, extracted into the game's package `port/games/christmas/` (SVG shapes, JSON symbols, MP3 sounds; made from the SWF when the game is built, and the SWF itself is not shipped). All art is drawn from its vectors, with no bitmaps.
- **The game runs on its own scripts**, its operation included. The menus, story, transitions, banners, credits, help, save, sounds and the operation (sprite 6233) are the original's ActionScript. FFDec decompiles it into the package's `scripts/`, `port/tools/index_scripts.py` translates it to actions, and the generic stage runs them (`src/surgeon/StageScreen.h`; [engine.md](engine.md#the-stage-a-game-run-by-its-own-scripts)).
- **One game with AS1.** `ChristmasGame` and `As1Game` are the same `FlashSurgeonGame` (`src/surgeon/`), each giving only what is its own: its SharedObject and save file, the pause screen's sound buttons, the online score parts to hide, its operation sprite, its patients, and its additions to the original. The debug probe (the play-tests' trace, the F1 menu's operation acts) is AS1's. Every engine fix made for AS1 is Christmas's too. The earlier native port of the operation is archived in `port/attic/christmas-native-operation/`; what it put right in the original is now done as AS1's is (below).
- **Not an emulator.** No SWF bytecode runs. Only the translated statements of the decompiled scripts run, on the engine's own scene, with Flash's built-in classes (`Sound`, `XML`, `SharedObject`) implemented by the engine.
- **Source.** The iOS version is not used as a reference.

The parts the games share live in `src/surgeon/`.

## Art: `VectorArt`

`VectorArt::library("library.json")` loads the game package's art and sounds once (a SWF itself also works, for tools).

**The package** is the movie extracted into categorized files ([engine.md](engine.md)): `extract_swf` writes `port/games/christmas/` from the SWF when the game is built, checks the round trip is exact, and the game reads it back into the same `flash::Swf` model the parser fills.

**Lookup:**
- `symbol("linkage")` (or `"<id>"`) finds a symbol;
- `frameOf(symbol, "label")` finds a labelled frame;
- `placement(sprite, frame, "name" | "#id", transform)` gives where a timeline (0: the main one) places a piece, so screens are drawn exactly where the Flash game placed them.

**Drawing.** `draw(symbol, frame, age, transform, owner, look)` (or `drawUnder` with a world matrix):
- Draws the symbol showing `frame` (0: by its own timeline).
- `age` is how long it has been on screen, in movie frames.
- The symbol's inner animations follow from that age. They obey the stop, goto and random-variant markers the artists put on their frames: the parser keeps only those plain frame actions (`flash::FrameAction`), and a frame script with any other logic keeps none.
- Each instance keeps its own random variant, with no flicker.
- A piece moved to a new character by its timeline keeps its matrix, colour, name and mask, as Flash did.
- Shapes, morph shapes and static texts are drawn by the GPU as triangles (`flash/VectorMesh`, cached per size step and morph step: [engine.md](engine.md#the-loop-and-the-display-three-threads)), sharp at any window size with no texture made; pieces under filters and text fields are rasterised at the size they appear at. Masks go through the stencil.
- **Smooth motion.** Each drawn piece is paired with itself in the pass before (by its place in the art), so the art's tweens and the game's motion both move evenly at the display's rate. A piece moves by its place in the art, not by its corners (`platform::artPiece`): showing the same drawing it glides even when another texture shows it (rasterised anew at another size during a zoom, or once a sharper one is made); one that changes drawing (a mouth, a blink, a pose) or is about to go rides with the clip holding it until its pass, then steps to its new drawing, as authored. The clip it rides with is that very instance (its path of depths and its Flash instance: `clipId` in VectorArt.cpp), never another clip placed at that depth since: AS1's bullet hole (and the magnet wound's), whose holder puts the closed cut at the hole's depth when stapled, slid off up and away, twice its size, on the cut's placement for that pass. Before, such a piece stood still until its pass while the pieces around it moved on: on a high-refresh display a face's mouth and eyes came apart from it in the intro's close-ups, and backgrounds hitched as they were rasterised anew (play-tested 2026-10-22). Drawings never slide into each other, and a morph (a shape tween: the intro's caption bands) steps at its frames. `VectorArt::smoothTweens` off brings back the older rule (only the transform the art is drawn under moves smoothly). Checked with `AS_FILM` and `tools/motion_check.py` (the intro: no stutter, jumps or uneven speed); the check follows pieces by their texture, so it does not see pieces of one figure drifting apart: compare `AS_FILM ...:img` steps with the next frame as well.
- `owner` identifies the drawer from pass to pass.

**Look overrides.** A `VectorLook` overrides parts of a symbol for one draw, by instance path (`santa.santa.head.mouth`, matched on its end; `/holder.hit` from the symbol itself; `holder.hit.#61` for an unnamed clip in `holder.hit`) or `#<character id>` for unnamed clips anywhere:
- the text of named text fields (or of fields bound to a variable, `_root.dScore` or `dScore`);
- the frame of inner clips;
- pieces hidden, moved, or raised above the rest (`swapDepths`);
- the state of buttons (up/over/down).

**The art's random scripts.** Where no script runs (a native module's own clips; neither AS1 nor Christmas has any now, the stage running every clip's scripts), a game can register what its art did at random:
- `setRandomStart(sprite, frame, choices, play)`: a frame that goes to one of several frames (`gotoAndStop(random(5) + 1)`, `gotoAndPlay("t" + random)`), the pick fixed per instance;
- `addRandomTrigger(parent, child, chance)`: a child played now and then by its parent's `onEnterFrame` (`if (random(20) == 1) smokeplume.play()`), the same for an instance from pass to pass;
- `setRepeat(sprite, frame, from, times)`: a frame that sends the clip back to `from` until it has played `times` times, then stops one frame on (the credits' clips counting their loops);
- `setFrameOverride(character, frame)`: every instance of a clip held on a frame (a frame script that read the game's state when it appeared, as the lights lit once mission 1's plug is zapped).

Frame labels are found whatever their case, as Flash did (`probesuitEXCITED` is `probesuitexcited`), and a `gotoAndStop` to a label a clip lacks leaves it where it is.

**Queries:**
- `hitTest(…, only)` and `hitTestPart(…)` test a point against the drawn shapes or a piece's box;
- `partBounds(…)` gives a piece's bounding box; as Flash's `getBounds`, it counts hidden pieces;
- `innerFrame`, `innerSymbol`, `partInfo` and `childClips` tell what an inner clip shows, where it is, and which clips it holds. `childClips` lists them as Flash's `for..in` over a clip met them: unnamed clips too (`#<id>`), the newest first (placed on a later frame, then the deeper);
- **Instances.** `partInfo` also gives the Flash instance at a path. It changes when that clip, or one above it, is placed anew by its timeline (its depth removed or placed again), even with the same character and name. Hidden or moved looks, part playheads and the operation's per-piece state (a hit piece's health, the pieces done or stapled) belong to an instance. Mission 2's eye swaps its black burn pieces for green cream pieces of the same names, and the new ones start shown, with no health;
- `buttonAt(…, look)` finds the topmost button under a point.

**Text.** `drawText(editText, text, x, y, scale, owner)` draws one of the movie's text fields with any text (the debug menu's labels).

**Sounds.** `sound("linkage")` decodes an exported sound once, to 16-bit samples shared by every track playing it (each Flash Sound object is its own track, `SoundEffectTrack::initShared`), as Flash played it: an MP3 from after its seek samples (the encoder's delay) for its sample count, so it has no silence at either end. A sample count under half the sound is plainly wrong and is ignored: AS1's tongs grab claims 154 samples (14 ms) of a 0.11 s effect, which the original played whole. A sound repeated (`start(0, times)`, the music) loops in the mixer, sample-exact, and its last play is let run out: no gap between plays.

**Rasterising.** What is still rasterised (filtered pieces, text fields, gradients under an added colour; everything with debug `AS_RASTER_ART=1`), drawing never waits for:
- **Prefetch.** `prefetch(...)` with the arguments of a later `draw` has that art made on worker threads ahead of time. The intro asks for the frame one second ahead, and has its first second made before it starts.
- **New sizes.** A new size of art already cached (a zoom, a window resize) is made in the background while the old size is drawn scaled.
- **Operation stages.** When an operation starts, every stage's background and injuries are queued as `BuildLevel` will draw them, and kept (up to 6 minutes) until drawn: a stage that opens another layer of the patient shows without rasterising.
- **Transitions.** While a transition still hides the screen (the main timeline's frame named by `_root.fTarg` not reached yet), the stage has that frame's art made in the background, and the finished art is handed to the texture thread 20 MB a pass instead of 4. The next screen then shows without rasterising. The stage also has its own art made a second ahead, as it plays on (the intro).
- **New art, side by side.** Art nobody asked for ahead (seen for the first time, no other size to stand in) is not made on the spot piece after piece: while a pass draws, such art is queued first in line for the workers (`Raster::deferring`) and left out; the pass then waits for it, the game's thread making some too (`finishDeferred`), and draws again with it all (`SurgeonGame::cycle`; drawing twice in a pass changes nothing else). A screen coming in has its new pieces made by the workers at once (all the processors but two, up to eight: a phone's eight cores make six at a time): made one after another, AS1's transitions took up to a tenth of a second a pass. Big art not tiled yet is still drawn whole at a coarse size meanwhile (below), that stand-in made with the rest.
- **Uploads.** Finished art goes to the texture thread ([engine.md](engine.md#the-loop-and-the-display-three-threads)): the game's thread only names the texture and hands its texels over, a few megabytes a pass (or at once when drawn), and a frame is shown once its textures are in. Art handed over ahead (not drawn before the next pass) gives that thread a pass longer. Art up to 256 texels a side goes in a slot of a shared page (`platform::atlasPut`), so that pieces drawn one after another are one draw; only bigger art has a texture of its own.
- **No mipmaps.** Art is rasterised at the size it is shown (at most 1.41× larger, scale steps being √2 apart), and the driver generating them stalled for hundreds of milliseconds on big textures.
- **Morph shapes** use 64 steps.
- **Memory.** The engine is meant to stay light: memory stays flat however long a game runs. AS1 at 600×460 holds about 250 MB in an operation, and about 480 MB at 1800×1380 (most of that is the GL driver's). Its story screens, zooming big art, go higher: about 1 GB of private memory at their peak at 1800×1380, settling near 850 MB, while the textures themselves stay near 120 MB (measured 2026-10-23; the rest is the GL driver's, which keeps what the tiles' turnover allocated). The rules below keep it so; with `AS_TRACE` set (not `-d` alone) the stage writes a census every 250 passes ([engine.md](engine.md)).
  - **Budget.** The cached textures are kept under 80 MB (`Raster::kBudget`), the shared pages' room no art holds counted too (art scattered over pages keeps them all). Art goes when it has not been drawn for 120 passes, or, past the budget, the least recently drawn first, the art in pages under half full before the rest (what leaves an emptying page lets it go). What was drawn in the last second stays, so a looping animation's frames are not made again and again (a stutter), unless the cache is past twice the budget; then only the last pass's art, and what was asked for ahead, stays.
  - **Tiles.** Art more than 256 texels wide or tall at its drawn size (and unfiltered) is made in tiles of 254 texels, and only the tiles on screen (with a twentieth of it around) are drawn. A ring a tenth wider is made in the background ahead of being needed, so art moving or zooming in rarely waits for a tile. Each tile has one texel of its neighbours' art around it, so tiles drawn side by side filter seamlessly. Art zoomed far in (AS1's holder background at 128×) or reaching far off screen costs no more than the part of the screen it covers.
  - **Tiles not made yet.** Tiles on screen that are not made yet (new art, a zoom to another size, a pan faster than the ring) are made in the background. Meanwhile the art is drawn from another size's tiles where those cover the screen, or else whole at a coarse size (at most 1024 texels a side, one walk of the art), made at once if need be. Each tile is a walk of the whole art: made at once, the screenful of tiles of a story screen's art (100-300 a pass) stuttered AS1's story and menus.
  - **Typed text.** A text field is made only over its lines (with a glyph's height of slack, within the field), not its whole box: AS1's story dialogue, typed a letter a pass, was a new texture of the whole box (2560×1280) every letter.
  - **Flat tiles.** A tile of one colour all over (the inside of a big flat fill) is a single texel stretched over its place, and a fully transparent one has no texture at all.
  - **Still clips as one picture.** A clip whose art has not changed for 10 passes, holding only shapes and morph shapes (at most faded, unfiltered, no masks), opaque itself, and big (its layers adding up to a quarter of the stage), is drawn as one picture wherever it moves as a whole, tiled like the rest (`VectorArt::drewStill`, `Raster::group`). An operation's background, layers of full-screen shapes over each other, is then one layer of textures instead of four or five. The picture is made in the background while the clip is still drawn piece by piece. A faded piece in it is drawn alone and then faded as one, so its own overlapping fills do not show through each other. A faded clip is not flattened, because Flash showed its overlapping pieces through each other. Any change to its art goes back to piece by piece. Small clips are never flattened: a cursor that shows the tool in hand (AS1's), or a button's states, would switch between the two whenever its art changed, and smooth motion pairs a picture with its pieces badly.
  - **A node's still picture is its slice.** A node drawn in slices around the clips made nodes in it (`ArtNode::drawSlice`) and flattened as a still picture (`drewStill`) is flattened slice by slice, its own symbol walked as instance 0 so the slice's depths apply: walked as its picture's key, every slice held the whole symbol, and AS1's brain was drawn again above its cut line (made a node by its `_alpha`).
  - **Recycled textures.** Texture sides are rounded up to one of eight sizes an octave (most are the tiles' 256). Art bigger than a page's slot has its own texture, and a dropped one's storage is kept (up to 16 MB, 120 frames) for the next art of the same size (`platform::recycleTexture`/`reuseTexture`). The GL driver's memory grows with every texture it allocates and is slow to give it back: AS1's poison swarms reached 16-22 GB in god-mode test runs before these rules.
  - **Morph detail under load.** Each tweening morph instance needs a new texture every pass. A swarm of big ones (AS1's expanded poison clouds, three morphs each) needed hundreds of MB a pass at full-screen sizes. While more than 8 MB of art is made a frame on average, morphs are made one scale step coarser (up to four, drawn scaled up), then with half the tween's steps (32, 16, then 8). They return to full detail a step at a time once under 2 MB a frame for 500 frames. Normal play stays at full detail.
- **Filters.** Flash's blur, glow (outer, inner, knockout) and drop shadow on a clip are baked into the textures of the pieces inside it:
  - **Per piece.** Each piece is filtered on its own; Flash filtered the clip's whole picture, which is the same for the credits' glowing and blurred texts, the tree lights' glow, the help pages' outlined text, the cream blobs' soft shadow and the bandage wipe's motion blur.
  - **A faded clip as one picture.** A filtered clip that is also faded (its alpha under 1, no added colour) and holds only shapes and morph shapes is rasterised whole (`Raster::group`, its pieces in place; cached by what is drawn where), filtered, then faded as one, as Flash drew it: faded piece by piece, its overlapping pieces showed through each other (the AS2 dove's high shadow, an inner-glow silhouette at 20%, was a patchwork of darker overlaps). A clip holding texts, masks, inner filters or coloured pieces is still drawn per piece. Until that picture is made (on worker threads, busy when a layer of the patient comes up), the clip is drawn per piece, not left out.
  - **Sizes.** They are sized in stage pixels and quantised, so a tweened filter does not make a texture per frame.
  - **Stand-ins.** While a new filtered version is made in the background, the art's last version is drawn.
  - **Lower resolution.** Art under a blur of 4, 8 or 16 screen pixels is rasterised at a lower resolution (cheaper, and the blur hides the detail).

## The surgeon game: `src/surgeon/`

- **`SurgeonGame`:** the canvas. It runs the current `Screen` (the game's `StageScreen`; update and draw in stage pixels, pointer and key input), the sounds, and the debug menu.
- **`StageScreen`, `Scene`, `Script`, `FlashClasses`:** the movie run by its own scripts ([engine.md](engine.md#the-stage-a-game-run-by-its-own-scripts)).
- **`ArtNode`:** a node of the scene, as a Flash clip was: a symbol with its own playhead, its place (position, rotation, scale, alpha, visibility, tint) and the nodes attached to it by depth (`attach`, `remove`, `swapDepths`). Its inner parts can be played or held (`partGotoAndStop`), hidden, moved, given text, and tested (`hitTest` against a point or, by bounding boxes, against another node's part). A part replaced by its timeline (the node or a part above went to another frame) starts over as a new instance. With `partClocks` (the stage's), what a goto places in a part starts then (the credits' animations).
- **`SoundBank`:** the operation's sounds by name. On the stage they go through the scripts' own `playSound` and `stopSound`, and its music and sound switches through the pause screen's buttons' scripts.
- **`DebugMenu`:** the F1 menu (debug mode): a panel of the game's buttons (`SurgeonGame::debugItems()`), drawn with the movie's own text field.
- **`SymbolViewer`:** debug only (`AS_VECTOR_SYMBOL`, `AS_VECTOR_FRAME`, `AS_VECTOR_AT`, `AS_VECTOR_SCALE`, `AS_VECTOR_TREE`). It shows one symbol on an empty stage, to compare with FFDec's renders (`ffdec-cli -format sprite:png -selectid <id> -export sprite <dir> <swf>`).

## Christmas: `src/christmas/`

**`ChristmasGame`** is a `FlashSurgeonGame` ([as1.md](as1.md#set-up-flashsurgeongame)) given Christmas's own:
- the SharedObject `user_9848...` kept as `christmas_save.txt` (its levels as `grade<n>`, no `balls`: the file the port always wrote);
- the pause screen's music and sound buttons (6112/6114, 6120/6122), the online score submission's button and label hidden (6351, 6352);
- its operation sprite (6233) and its five missions; `pressed`, a `_root` flag only its scripts keep (mission 4's second scene shown);
- its additions (`StageExtras.*`), **not in the Flash game**, asked for by its players:
  - `LevelsBack` (5665): the level select's BACK tag (the credits' button 4251) and Escape, back to the title.
  - `WorkshopEngine` (5688): in the workshop scenes after the first, the van's engine stays where it crashed. The original's later backdrop has its own copy, never played.

**The pointer.** In an operation the game's cursor (the tool in hand) is the pointer: the system's is hidden while it is on screen (`Screen::drawsPointer`). The Flash game showed both. The cursor node is drawn as `platform::PointerArt` (`ArtNode::pointer`), where the mouse is at every display step, as AS2's and AS3's are.

## Restored sounds

The user's restored sounds are in the package in place of the movie's, put there by the build (`extract_swf ... --sound <export>=<file>`, as AS1's: [as1.md](as1.md#restored-music)):
- `music restored/asx/xmasmusico.mp3` replaces `xmasmusico` (the title and menus' music);
- every `music restored/asx/sfx/<linkage>.wav` (or `.mp3`) replaces the sound exported by that name: Christmas's own `cream_s`, `stapler_s`, `bear1_s` and `bear2_s` (16-bit PCM WAV, kept as the movie's raw sound);
- every other sound Christmas shares with AS1 is AS1's restored one: the 33 effects its movie exports that `asx` has none of (`music restored/as1/sfx/<linkage>.mp3`: the tools, the tray, the van, the street...), and the operation's music `operation_s` (`music restored/as1/operation.mp3`). The rest of the sounds both movies have (the screams, `death_s`, `congrat_s`, `warehouse_s`...) are the same recordings in both, and AS1 has no restored take of them.

A file added to either folder is picked up when cmake runs again (`CONFIGURE_DEPENDS`); the build re-extracts the package when one changes. AS1's `title.mp3` (its `theme_s`) has no counterpart in Christmas, whose title music is `xmasmusico`. Its scripts also attach four sounds neither movie has (`click_s`, `click2s`, `planeexplode_s`, `rollovers`): silent, as in the original.

## The operation: by its own scripts

Sprite 6233 ("game": frame 2 sets up, frame 3 holds the functions, frame 5 is "lost") runs by its translated scripts on the stage, as AS1's operation does ([as1.md](as1.md), [engine.md](engine.md#running-the-scripts)): no code of the port's own plays it. Until 2026-10-23 it was a native C++ port (`port/attic/christmas-native-operation/`). What that port put right in the original is done now as for AS1:
- **By the engine, for every game:** Flash's comparisons with NaN (`undefined <= 0` true: a scorpion dies after three saw hits, an untouched piece takes the cream at once); frames a clip picks by `_root.level` (its own frame script runs: the cutter's blood trail no longer flashes between two drawings); clips acting on their parent (a nest ready for its next shock); NaN positions ignored; frame scripts run after the code that placed the clip, and before it is drawn (mission 5's hand puzzle and the skin made with it); a death final (the probe holds health at 0 once the runner dies).
- **Corrected in its scripts** (`games/christmas/scripts/`, as AS1's: [as1.md](as1.md#corrections-to-the-originals-scripts)):
  - **Only ice melts** (`DefineSprite_6233/frame_3/DoAction_17.as`, the lighter): on missions 3 and 4 the lighter melted every piece to take out but the presents into a cut and water (mission 4's phial and its broken halves too); now only ice (a piece on an `ice...` frame) or a frozen ant (`fant`).
  - **A tool let go anywhere ends** (`DoAction_3.as`, `onMouseUp`): the tool in hand ended only when let go off the buttons, unpaused, with the sketch put away, so a tool dragged onto a card or the dialogue strip (or paused with Esc while held) played its loop on for good, and a piece carried by the tongs onto the dialogue strip over the tray was neither dropped nor put back. Now every release ends it (AS1's the same).
  - **The corkscrew's yank** (`DoAction_28.as`): played on every release while the corkscrew was in hand; now only after a press that used it (AS1's the same).
- **Kept from the port's additions:** the level select's BACK tag and the workshop's engine (above).
- **Operation dialogue** is the Flash game's: many lines in mission 1, one in missions 2 and 5, none in missions 3 and 4 (their arrays are empty).

## Testing

**Reference: the original running in Ruffle.** `port/tools/ruffle_ref.py <swf> <outdir> <script> --files port/games/christmas/data` plays the original SWF, with its own scripts, in Ruffle (`tools/ruffle/ruffle.exe`, an open-source Flash player). It drives it with a script of `wait`, `click`, `move`, `down`/`up`, `glide`, `drag` and `shot`/`shots` lines (stage pixels), and saves the stage's pixels as references for the native screens.
- Ruffle's window must stay visible: the tool captures the screen.
- FFDec's renders are not a reference for animation: they draw nested clips frozen on their first frame.
- To reach the first operation: `wait 4`, `click 190 420` (New Game), `wait 5`, `click 572 432` (skip the intro), `wait 3`, `click 540 432` (skip the scene), `wait 5`, `click 530 425` (START).
- `--so <name> k=v,...` starts the movie with a saved shared object, so a later patient can be reached: Continue (`click 390 420`), the patient's card, SKIP (`click 570 445`), START. Christmas's is `user_9848945498456554546545454`, and `level1=1,score1=100,timer1=100` marks mission 1 done.
- `at <seconds>` (from the start, or from a `mark` line) keeps a long script on time.
- `down`, `up` and `drag` move the pointer first: Ruffle takes a press where it last saw the pointer.

**The native game:**

Use the usual automation (`AS_SHOT` frame numbers, 25 per second; `AS_CLICKS`, `AS_DRAG` with paths, `AS_KEYS` for F1 and number keys) with `AS_SAVE_DIR`, in debug mode (`-d`).
- `AS_HIDDEN=1` keeps the test window off the desktop.
- `AS_WINDOW=<w>x<h>` sets the window's size.
- `AS_PROFILE=1` logs each pass's update and draw times, the art rasterised on the spot, and the texture uploads.
- `AS_SHOT_T=0.5` shoots the smooth-motion display halfway between passes.
- `AS_TRACE=1` logs, in an operation:
  - each injury created, changing stage or removed, with its number (`id`), body level, and where its glass (and its box) and hit pieces are on the stage; where each critter (ant, spider, scorpion...) has moved to; injuries not made and why;
  - the mission stage and body level when they change;
  - a puzzle's pieces and slots, each organ put on the tray (and for which injury), each organ dropped on its place (in or missed);
  - the heart stopping and starting again, the patient lost (and why), the mission won;
  - at each tool press, what it meets: the injury, its hidden pieces, the patient's health, the blood the vacuum holds, the tray's box;
  - where the corkscrew's or vacuum's moving hit is, each frame it is in use.

  This is what an automated test aims at.
- `AS_CONTROL=1` drives the game live from stdin. At each frame a `run <n>` reached it prints `@frame <n>` and reads commands: `click`, `drag`, `key` as above with absolute frames; `down`, `move`, `up <x> <y>` for the pointer now; `hover <x> <y>` (the pointer moved, not pressed); `key <frame>:left|right|up|down` holds that arrow for the frame; `text <chars>` presses those keys now (`charKey`); `shot <file>`; `run <n>`; `quit`. A script reads the trace as the game runs and aims each move at what it reports.

**Slow passes.** In debug mode a pass over 80 ms is written to `debug.log` (`slow pass <n> (<ms>): update, draw (art made at once, art uploaded at once), swap-in; display scale`), so a hitch on a player's machine shows where it went; a display frame late by over 50 ms is too (`slow display frame`). A pass now has its whole length (40 ms) before the display notices it (the display thread, [engine.md](engine.md#the-loop-and-the-display-three-threads)); `AS_DISPLAY_LOG` and `tools/display_pace.py` check the display's pace itself. Fully transparent pieces (the body layers not shown yet) ask for no texture.

**On the stage, `AS_TRACE=1` also logs:**
- `stage <label>`: each frame the main timeline goes to;
- `button <id> <event> on <path>`: each button handler run, and the clip it ran on;
- `progress saved: level <n> grade <g>`: the operation's result written to the scripts' save;
- `script: nothing answers <name>()`: a call no script, engine class or module answers.

`AS_TRACE_VARS=a,b` prints every set of those variables.

**Play-tests** (`port/tools/flash_playtest.py [scenario...]`) play the operations with the real tools and check the trace for the progress a player should make. Run them after every change to the operation or the stage. Never count a stage as working because the debug menu's skip got past it.
- Scheduled scenarios (`m1_*`, `m2_*`) give fixed input by frame number, each press with the pointer moved onto its point a frame before (`AS_HOVER`), as a player's: the stage's buttons and the scripts' cursor are where the pointer last moved.
- Live scenarios use `AS_CONTROL`:
  - `story2` starts from a save with mission 1 done: Continue, the Mincer's card, its scene skipped, the operation played, its won scenes and banner, and back on the level select checks that the grade marks hold still (they once kept rolling there, the game stuck).
  - `death_final` kills the patient and heals them a few frames into the flatline: the lost board must still come.
  - `release_over_card` drags the lighter, cream and cutter onto a tool card and lets go there: each one's loop must stop.
  - `story` plays the game through its own menus, as a player would. It starts a new game, skips the intro and the story scene, presses START, plays mission 1 to the end, then goes through its won scenes and banner back to the level select, with the grade saved.
  - `mission1`…`mission5` play a whole operation, every stage, to "mission won" (`play_mission()`).
  - `m3_ice` and `m3` play mission 3's first stages. `clear()` plays whatever is on the current body level until the stage is done:
  - it works each injury with its stage's tool from `GameData`: the chainsaw or cutter along its hit pieces, the stapler on them, the lighter and cream worked over them, the tongs pulling its glass out along its way (within 30° of its rotation − 90°, slowly) and on to the tray, or bringing its organ from the tray to its place, a puzzle's pieces to their slots, the vacuum on water's and soot's drops;
  - each stroke moves one frame at a time and lets go as soon as the trace shows the progress it was for, as a player stops when the stage changes;
  - presses stay in the field (65–535 × 105–395), off the HUD and the dialogue strip;
  - it takes the small injuries first;
  - critters are hit where they are now (the trace follows them), as often as they need;
  - it puts Pixie dust in with the corkscrew when the patient's health runs low, following the screw round, and starts a stopped heart with the battery;
  - a press that does not reach the tool (the dialogue strip over the tray) clicks the strip away first.

  A whole mission takes a few minutes; run the five side by side.
- Each run leaves `trace.txt`, `actions.txt` (the input, by frame) and its screenshots in a temporary folder. `port/tools/xmas_ruffle_replay.py <that folder> <out>` plays the same input on the original in Ruffle, with a save marking the earlier missions done, timed at 25 frames a second from the operation's start. It saves the same screenshots: compare them stage by stage.

**The debug menu** (F1, debug mode) starts any screen or mission (a mission straight from the title or the intro stops their music and the van, as the screens a player comes through do), and in an operation skips the stage (its injuries removed, so the game's own stage logic runs), wins, heals, adds time, kills the patient, or keeps the patient alive (god mode). Its buttons sit in rows from (12, 40), 112×26 pixels with 6 between: Title, Intro, Levels; Mission 1–4; Mission 5; Skip stage, Win now, Heal, +60 s; Kill patient; God mode, Smooth, Unlock all.
