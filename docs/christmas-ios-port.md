# The archived iOS-based Christmas port

**Archived.** This was Amateur Surgeon Christmas ported from its iOS app, the same way as AS1. It has been replaced by the Christmas rebuilt from its Flash original ([christmas.md](christmas.md)), which now builds as `AmateurSurgeonXmas`. Its sources, assets and asset tools are kept, unbuilt, in `port/attic/xmas-ios/` (`src-xmas/`, `assets-xmas/`, `tools/`). To build it again, move `src-xmas` back to `port/src/xmas` and `assets-xmas` to `port/assets-xmas`, and add to `port/CMakeLists.txt`: `add_game(AmateurSurgeonXmasIOS src/xmas res/AmateurSurgeonXmas.rc assets-xmas surgeonxmas-ios)`. Its asset tools import each other from `port/tools/`. Its save was `RMS.txt` in the same Saved Games folder as the new game's.

What follows is its documentation as it stood.

**Amateur Surgeon Christmas** (`src/xmas/`, `AmateurSurgeonXmas.exe`): every screen and all five patients are ported; under play-testing. At the user's request it is restyled after AS1 (next section).

## Christmas on AS1's screen

Port changes, asked for by the user after play-testing, that make Christmas look like Amateur Surgeon 1 rather than the iPhone original (`src/xmas/Layout.h`):

- **4:3 screen.** The game runs on AS1's 1024×768 screen. Each panel keeps a layout space (`GamePanel::layoutWidth/Height`, 480×320 unless it says otherwise), fitted into the screen at a uniform scale and centred: `clearContext` sets the projection to it, `GameCanvas` converts touches into it, and `clearLayout` clears the bars around it black. Menus, story and other 3:2 screens are letterboxed. Operations use 480×360: their 720×480 scene is drawn at 3/4, filling the height and losing 30 layout pixels at each side.
- **AS1's HUD layout in operations.** The JogLog and the pause button sit top left, and the ten tools are one row of the Christmas cards (drawn at 0.58) along the top, with the hint arrows under them pointing up. The original had two columns of five down the sides. Timer, help, score and dialogue stay along the bottom (moved down 40).
- **AS1's widgets.** The JogLog (`LifeMeter`), the vacuum gauge, the corkscrew and the scan board are AS1's: its iPad pictures, copied to `assets-xmas/images-as1/` by `xmas_hd.py`, placed with AS1's own coordinates and drawn at AS1's size (the JogLog under a `kAS1` scale, the effects at ratio `kAS1`, the scan board sliding in at AS1's speed). Their behaviour stays Christmas's. Swapping single pictures does not work for these: an AS1 wedge in the Christmas corkscrew ring, or AS1's JogLog frame around the Christmas display, did not line up.
- **AS1's sizes.** Measured on the same 1024×768 screen, most Christmas HUD pieces and prompts were bigger than AS1's: the vacuum gauge 1.9×, the corkscrew marker 1.6×, the pop-up grade words 1.45×, the score board 1.35×, the hint arrows 1.3×, and the timer, help, pause button, dialogue text and JogLog 1.1–1.2×. Each is now drawn at AS1's size, and the help button, score board and pause button also at AS1's positions. `layout::kAS1` (480/1024) converts AS1 pixels to layout pixels. Groups of images and texts (JogLog, timer, score board, a word, the dialogue's text and speaker) are scaled about their anchor corner with `layout::ProjectionScope`, which works on the projection because `TextDrawer` resets the modelview. The dialogue strip is AS1's (from y 638 down), with the text at 0.8 and wrapped wider. The instrument tray, the scene and the story screens were already at AS1's proportions. The menus, patient selection and lose screen are Christmas's own compositions and keep their layout. The pause and help phones keep their 480×320 layout, centred (help is shifted in the projection, because its texts reset the modelview). The scan board is drawn at 9/8 to cover the taller layout. The zoom (magnifier) buttons are removed, so the scene no longer pans.
- **Full-screen effects.** The red "miss" flash is drawn over the whole screen, not under the scene's scale. The loading screen clears its own projection instead of inheriting the operation's.
- **HD art from AS1.** `python port/tools/xmas_hd.py` looks, for every Christmas image, for the AS1 picture it was made from: by name (`ipad_` first), then by content among all larger AS1 images (renamed files, such as the dialogue faces or the cut pictures `is_08`/`is_10`). A candidate counts when its visible part has the same shape and, scaled down and compared blurred (so a re-crop, a pixel of offset or a slight turn do not count), the same outline, the same structure and the same colours after an affine colour fit (recolouring is allowed). Accepted ones are written to `assets-xmas/images-hd/` at twice the Christmas size, recoloured with the fit when that is closer. The matches were checked by eye (`--sheet` saves them side by side). The script lists the cases the test gets wrong, with the reason. Excluded are Probe's clean-coat portraits, the name card with Mincer's name written on it, and the pictures of the AS1 widgets above. Forced is the hint arrow. 53 images match, among them the timer, the help button, the score board, all the grade words, cuts, fire, ice, organs, nests, kryptonite and the dialogue faces. The rest is Christmas's own art, or AS1's version is drawn differently (tool cards, menus, the timer tab), so it keeps its original resolution. Re-run the script after re-extracting either game's assets.
- **Vector art from the Flash original.** Amateur Surgeon Christmas began as a Flash game (Mediatonic, 2008). Its SWF, preserved by the Flashpoint archive (`re/flash/`), holds all the art as vector shapes. Pictures that come from it are drawn from those vectors, so they are sharp at any window size.
  - **The game side.** An `images-svg/<name>.svg` replaces the image `<name>` (`ResourceTexture`). lunasvg (`third_party/lunasvg-*`, MIT) rasterises it at the smallest whole multiple of the image's size that matches the display: the game's `GameInfo::artScale` (Christmas: 1024/480 screen units per art pixel) times `platform::displayScale()` (window pixels per screen unit). The texture is mipmapped and reported at the image's own size, and it is rasterised again whenever the display scale changes (`platform::onDisplayScaleChange`), for example on going fullscreen.
  - **Composing the SVGs: `port/tools/swf_svg.py`.** JPEXS FFDec (`tools/ffdec_*`, needs Java) exports every shape, text and button exactly as SVG. Its SVG of a whole symbol, though, leaves out Flash's colour transforms and masks. `swf_svg.py` therefore composes symbols itself from the SWF's timelines, with FFDec's leaves:
    - the display list at any frame, with placement matrices;
    - colour transforms baked into the colours, alpha as opacity;
    - clip layers as SVG clip paths;
    - nested clips at their own frame. A clip runs from when it was placed, and one placed on its parent's first frame and kept throughout keeps running across the parent's loops, as the Flash player does. Frame scripts are not run; FFDec does not run them either, and the results agree with its renders.
  - **The images: `python port/tools/xmas_flash.py render | match | build`.** `render` draws every shape and symbol frame at a low zoom with FFDec into a cache (`%TEMP%/xmas_flash`), leaving out cut scenes over 400 frames. `match` pairs each Christmas image with a render using `xmas_hd.judge`, taking the closest in colour of the frames that pass; the pairs are kept in `re/flash/xmas/matches.tsv`. `build` composes each match as SVG and renders it with `svgrender` (a small tool built with the game: lunasvg, as the game draws it). It checks the render against the image, places the vectors over the image's own content, applies the fitted colour transform to every colour where the iOS picture's colours differ, checks the placed SVG once more, and writes it to `images-svg/`.
  - **Result.** Run `xmas_flash.py` after `xmas_hd.py`; an SVG takes precedence over an `images-hd` PNG. 116 Christmas images are vectors: the light strings, organs, ribs and rib pieces, presents, patient cards, buttons and tags, grade words, Santa, the sleigh, the bomb and more. `EXCLUDE` lists the matches found wrong by eye, with the reason: a dotted guide or a sample combo count drawn on the Flash version, the pause phone's texts missing, a lighter rollover colour.
- **The story intro, native.** New Game played `storyOpening.m4v`, the Flash game's intro pre-rendered at 480×320 and 15 frames a second. The port plays the animation itself instead.
  - **The data.** `python port/tools/xmas_intro.py` takes symbol 5303 from the SWF (840 frames at 25 a second on the 600×460 stage). It writes it with `swf_svg` as one SVG, `assets-xmas/flash/intro.svg`: every shape once, wrapped as `<g id="u_<key>">` in its `<defs>`, and each frame as a hidden group `<g id="f<n>" data-defs="...">` listing the definitions it uses.
  - **Playback.** `engine/VectorMovie` is a `MovieSource` for `MoviePlayer`, alongside the video decoder. It puts together a small SVG of each frame and rasterises it with lunasvg on worker threads, 12 frames ahead, covering the screen at the display's resolution. A frame takes about 10 ms at 1024×768 and 30 ms at 1878×1440, against 330 ms when the whole document was rendered.
  - **Sound.** The Flash game starts the intro's sounds from its scripts, so the soundtrack is the iOS video's (`flash/intro.mp3`, the same mix).
  - **Fallback and skipping.** `MainMenu` plays the vector intro when it is there and the iOS video otherwise. The Flash version's SKIP tag shows; a click or Escape skips, as before. `AS_MOVIE_SHOT=<prefix>:<seconds>,...` takes screenshots during a movie (debug mode).
- **Inner detail in matching.** `xmas_hd.judge` also compares the detail inside the outline: where either picture has some, the amount must be similar (ratio of at least 0.6) and correlated (0.8). Without that, a plain silhouette, or one layer of a layered Flash symbol (a white rib without its shading, a sky without its scene), passed for the whole picture.
