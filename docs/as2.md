# Amateur Surgeon 2 HD — the port

`AmateurSurgeon2.exe`, ported from the iPad app (`IPAs/Amateur Surgeon 2 HD v1.2 iPad os32most_uniQue.ipa`; analysis in [amateur-surgeon-2-ipad.md](amateur-surgeon-2-ipad.md)). The IPA is ported whole; parts of the Flash original (`re/flash/as2/`) are taken in one by one on the user's request ([plan.md](plan.md), step 6), each described below where it replaced the iPad's.

## How it is built

| Part | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| Package | `port/games/as2/` | the IPA's content by `port/tools/extract_ipa.py`, plus the port's own heart-monitor frames by `port/tools/brain_dial.py` (run after it) (images; audio (its three CAF sounds kept, decoded by the engine); video; `anim/levels.txt`; `data/` the cut scenes and `Tutorials.plist`; `ui/` the 23 nibs as JSON, by `nib2json.py`), and `data/library.json` by `port/tools/as2_data.py` (the binary's catalogue and stage tables; the Flash AS2's stage tables, `flashStages`, from its decompiled injury classes, and where its levels placed each injury, `flashLevels`, from its levels.txt; the port's own injuries, `PORT_INJURIES` in the script, ids from 200: the dead dove, wasp, squid and small centipede, `Remains`) |
| Game | `port/src/as2/` | the binary's classes mirrored, one C++ class per Objective-C class, names kept (`GameController`, `Game`, `GameState`, `Sprite`, `SceneManager`, the menus' view controllers...) |
| Tables | `port/src/as2/GameData.{h,cpp}` | the binary's static tables (patient names, grade scores, the loader's sound and texture lists, tool names...), generated by `re/scripts/gen_tables.py as2` |
| Engine | `port/src/engine/ui/` | the UIKit subset AS2's screens need, general for any game (see [engine.md](engine.md#uikit-views-from-nibs)) |
| Decompile | `re/as2/` | `ann/<Class>.c`, read with `RE_GAME=as2 python re/scripts/view.py <Class>`; `ann/_fake_literals.txt` names leftover `DAT_f…` pointers |

Run it from `port/build-dev/AmateurSurgeon2.exe`; it finds `port/games/as2`. It is packed as `dist/surgeon2/` (`AmateurSurgeon2_dist`) and for Android as `dist/surgeon2-android/AmateurSurgeon2.apk` (`port/tools/android/build_apk.py`, [port.md](port.md#building-and-running)). The save is `%USERPROFILE%\Saved Games\Amateur Surgeon 2\UserDefaults.txt` (the original's `NSUserDefaults` keys: `Score<n>`, `Time<n>`, a tool's name for its level, `Tool<n>`, `SoundFXOn`, `MusicOn`, `ResumeGameAvailable`).

## The original, and how the port runs it

- **Loop.** A `CADisplayLink` called `GameController updateScene:` and `renderScene` once per display frame at the EAGLView's frame interval: 1 (60 a second) for the splash and loading, 2 in a mission (30 a second), 30 in the menus (only UIKit moves there). The port runs the platform's passes at 60 a second, or 30 in a mission (`platform::setPassSeconds`), so a mission's motion is smoothed between its 30 updates. At interval 30, `renderScene` still draws every pass (it has no loading to do then).
- **Screen.** The iPad build draws the iPhone's 480×320-point layout at twice the size: every shape's position and offset are translated twice (`SceneManager renderScene`), its quad in the HD art's pixels. So the world takes the top-left 960×640 of the 1024×768 screen; touches are halved into it (`Panel touchesBegan:`). The menus are UIKit views from the nibs, 1024×768; their 960×640 backgrounds are stretched to fill (`PCGImageView`).
- **Orientation.** The iPad's window was portrait; the GL scene and every view were turned to landscape. The port's screen is landscape: the turns are left out, and the transitions' directions (given for the portrait window) are turned too (`MenuManager`: top → right, left → top).
- **Drawing.** Textures are premultiplied and drawn with `(GL_ONE, GL_ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA)` as the original's were; the shapes are recorded under the GL matrices the original set, so the platform smooths them. Opaque images, which the device kept as RGB565, keep all their colour. Textures clamp at their edges; the original left GL's default, repeating, so the filtering at a quad's edge blended in the picture's opposite edge (faint lines along the HUD dial's right and bottom edges in a window larger than 1024×768). A picture drawn longer than itself tiles (`Texture2D::tile`: an extraction's long object such as the clown's scarf, the insertion's pin).
- **Reading the decompile.** A double argument shows as two words, the high one printed as a float: `setDuration_(a, 0, 1.625f)` is 0x3FD00000_00000000, **0.25 s**; `2.5625f` is 10 s, `2.0625f` 2.5 s, `1.875f` 1 s, `1.8125f` 0.75 s.

## Removed, changed, added

- **Removed:** OpenFeint, Game Center (leaderboards, achievements, the total-score post), Bango analytics, "More Games", the developers' debug switch. Their buttons are hidden.
- **Changed (only the screen):** the intro movie is the 960×640 `MovieHi.m4v` (the iPad build chose the 480×320 one); system fonts stand in for iOS's (Arial for Helvetica, Segoe Print for Marker Felt).
- **Heart attack (not in the iPad game):** a heart driven to 200 or more (the injection's shots add without limit) drops to 0 at once (`heart attack` in the trace, a red flash), and a heart at 0, however it got there, is stopped for good (`GameState::heartStopped`): no shot (no "healed" either) and no battery restarts it, the death animation runs out and the mission fails. Not in the graveyard (mission 20, whose risen patient starts at 0), nor for the vampire (mission 10, no beat), nor in god mode.
- **Added (not in the iPad game):** in debug mode (`-d`; `port/build-play` has a launcher, `AmateurSurgeon2 (debug).bat`) F1 opens the debug menu (`engine/ui/DebugPanel`, a panel of buttons over the window; its items in `AS2App::debugItems`): missions 1–30 straight into the operation (from the menus), and in a mission win now, next stage (every injury taken away), heal, +60 s, lose and god mode (the heart and the clock kept up, no shock, a stopped heart no death, no heart attack; a brain out keeps its countdown); smooth motion on or off; unlock all (each unplayed mission given a score of 1 in the save in use). `AS_GOTO=<mission>:<stage>` (debug mode) goes from the main menu straight into that mission's stage (the stages before it taken away, as "Next stage"), for screenshots; `AS_GOD` (debug mode) turns god mode on from the start (a probe that shoots at a monster for screenshots cannot lose the patient meanwhile); "Next stage" takes away only the stage's own injuries (the next stage's, made as the last went, stay). Escape skips a movie (a double tap still does) and in a mission presses pause; the number keys pick the tools (1–9, 0 for the tenth), as the Flash AS2's did. The torch of the dark stages (missions 9 and 10) followed the iPad's tilt: its light sits off the middle by the tilt times the screen (half a g reaches the edge), the readings smoothed 0.95 old to 0.05 new at 100 a second. On a device with an accelerometer it still does (`GameController::readTilt` each pass from `platform::deviceTilt`, the same smoothing scaled to the pass length; the light goes toward the lower edge); without one (a PC) it follows the pointer (`GameController::pointerAt` sets the tilt that puts the light under it). **Shaking** (the ticks, the big tick, the snake: `GameController mShakeDetected`, read each pass by `updateShake`): with an accelerometer as the original (`platform::deviceAcceleration`; excited once two axes change by more than 0.54 g between readings, calm again under 0.49 g); without one, the pointer shaken hard while not pressed (`GameController::readShake`: strokes of 30 screen points or more a pass (30 passes a second in a mission, so about 900 points a second) turned back on themselves four times, each within 10 passes of the last, else it starts over and calms; in practice a swing of ±200 points once a second, ±120 one and a half times or ±80 twice, counted in about 0.6 to 2 seconds, while small or slow wiggles never count; a first measure of 15 points and two reversals was set off by any brisk move of the mouse; pressed, it works a tool, and a chainsaw sawing to and fro had stunned the Overlord, the big tick, the moment it flew free). **A tilt held by hand** (the transistor's needle, kept on its gauge while the other hand stitches): the device's, or on a PC the arrow keys (`GameController::balance`, `arrowKey`: held, the tilt moves 1/128 a pass, up to 1/8 either way); the pointer is busy with the tool, so it does not steer this one. The black around the light is four quads; the original placed them on the screen by their corners, which between passes stepped while the light glided (a gap opened on its leading side as it moved), so here they are placed about the light's own position, reaching far past the screen, and glide with it.

  - The torch tutorial's "Tilt the iPad": "the device" on a device, and on a PC "Move the pointer: the Flashlight follows it" (`GameState::getTutorialText`).
  - Cut scenes (missions 1–20): the character slots' pictures stand on the slot's bottom corner, as the nib's content mode (bottom left/right) meant; the original kept the slot's top, so the pig hung in the air.
  - The type 'n' tell (mission 12): the cords' top and bottom pieces at their places (172 above, 135 below; they were left at the middle, over the device), the bomb on the screen's left (where the word runs into it), the word shown only inside the screen, and on a PC the keys typed by the keyboard (`TouchSink::charKey`: the key as the layout and shift make it).
  - Jimmy the Spider (16): an egg let go anywhere but the tray hatches where it falls, as the original's tongs did (the port only put it back).

  - Dr Bleed (20): the dirt patches are not drawn (the original made them but never put them in the scene; shown, nothing could clear them off the coffin's line).

  - Elephant Boy (26): the peanut splatter's picture ("peanutSplatter") is in no build, so the loose peanuts never showed; the bundle's unused peanut pieces (`nut1`–`nut5`: kernels, broken shells, the open half-shell) stand in, one at random each, at the whole peanut's size (the level's are 128 points across, as the original's four-argument init made them).
  - Brain in a Jar (29): the brain's hole under the amp's flap (the original put it 26 points left of the flap and its cut line).
  - Ringmaster Pt2 (30): lifting out the old brain starts the heart running down (`GameState::loseBrain`): from where it is to 0 over 18 seconds, nothing else moving it meanwhile, the seconds left shown on the clock and a crossed-out brain on the heart monitor (`brainout0001`–`0016`, the port's own, made by `port/tools/brain_dial.py` after the game's crossed heart (`shocked0028`–`0041`): the brain, the game's top-down one as a silhouette, swells out of the standing jogger, pops, and two strokes go through it, behind it, in the heart's order and places, shrunk on the dial as the heart is: rerun it after `extract_ipa.py`, which clears `games/as2/images`); at 0 it dies as any heart. The original stopped the heart at once and failed the mission 825 passes (27.5 seconds) later, a limit that showed nowhere (the port at first had left the stop out, so the new brain's battery could never restart it: stuck). The battery then switches off, as held on it shocked the revived patient a frame later. That battery stage has no line: the whole new brain is its target (the original drew the cream's green line again, so it looked as if the cream did nothing).
  - Ringmaster Pt2's pupils (30) are shown at their own layer: a new part of the body moved them to the fading old layer with everything else, and they came back there, so after the brain the eyes were empty.
  - Transplants with a replacement waiting on the tray (the ribs, 21): the tongs take nothing else until it is put in, as the original's (the port let a second rib go to the tray).

## A mission

- **Game** (`Game.cpp`) runs a mission's frame (`checkRender`): the clock, the shock, `GameState render`, the effects, the tool in hand (`ITool render`). A stage ends when its injuries are gone (`checkInjuries`); a new part of the body fades the old art out (`needToFade`, `SceneManager MoveAllToOldLayer`). Won: the time bonus, the best score and time saved, an upgrade won on a first win. `findCorrectInjury` finds what the tool is on: an injury whose stage takes that tool, a box of it under the finger (the box turned with its guide sprite); `getAllHealedInjury` treats those boxes by the tool's heal amount.
- **GameState** holds the heart's state (the dial's runner state and picture, its beep, the death), the stage's background, health and time, the injuries and blood, the red flash, and the tutorials (`Tutorials.plist`: an event opens one; its actions unlock tools, point arrows, or chain the next).
- **Classes by name.** Tools, injuries, effects and view controllers are made by their class name as the game did (`NSClassFromString`; `Registry.cpp` registers the ported ones). `isKindOfClass:` tests use the binary's class tree (`library.json` `"classes"`), so subclasses count as the original's did.
- **Injuries** (`injuries/`) are the original's classes: `InjuryGraphic` (damage over time, the special: bleeding or a new cut), the cuts (`SmallCut`, `Incision`, `LargeCut`: a column of boxes down the cut), `Splatter`/`BloodSplatter` (drops that fly and land as stains). Each stage's tool, damage and look come from the binary's stage tables (`library.json` `"stages"`).
- **Tools** (`tools/`): `Cutter`, `Lighter`, `Cream` (`Tools.cpp`), `Stitch` (a stitch where the finger crosses the cut's middle line; enough boxes stitched closes the stage), `Tongs` (pull an object out along its direction, carry it to the tray), `Scanner` (the ULTRASOUND 5000: a pulse at the finger coloured by the nearest hidden injury's distance, green within 50 points to red past 150; an arrow to it from level 2, rings on every hidden one at level 3), `Vacuum` (treats its injuries' boxes under the nozzle, else sucks landed drops of blood and the other splatters: drawn in within its radius, taken within the nozzle's; `Splatter::suck`), `Injection` (now the Flash AS2's maze syringe, under the HUD below: the injury pressed on is treated, or the heart gains 10/13/16 by level), `Battery` (a tap shocks the creature under it; with nothing there the patient is shocked: a burn, the heart stopped, `inShock`, and 300 frames to revive them with another tap, the heart restarting at no more than 10/20/30 by level), `Chainsaw` (saws the boxes under it, shaking the screen and throwing blood; all of an injury's, or of one transplant part's, spent move it on; running over nothing wears the heart by damage/70 a frame); `ITool` holds what they share (turning on, treating the injuries under the finger, a wrong use's harm).
- **SurfaceExtraction** (`injuries/Extraction.cpp`): an object in a wound (its picture and lengths by its library linkage), pulled out, carried away, then the wound stitched, burnt and creamed. The tongs hold its `removable` (`DraggableWrapper`). **UnderSurfaceExtraction** is the same object under the skin: hidden (only the scanner finds it) until the cutter opens it at once; then it shows and bleeds (a poisonous one splashes poison and leaves some), and goes on as a surface extraction (6 stages). Its one box serves the cut and the pull.
- **Transplant** (`injuries/Transplant.cpp`): a diseased part replaced. Each part has a dashed cut line (its `hits`, shown yellow) sawn with the chainsaw; its piece, cut loose, is taken with the tongs to the tray, which then holds the healthy one (`Tongs` `trayHolding`); taken from the tray and let go within 16 points of its place it goes in, and the cut is burnt and creamed along the same line (black, then green). `findCorrectInjury` reads a transplant's stage tools as a list (`"chainsaw,tongs"`). **BoneTransplant** covers the pelvis, shoulder, spine, knee and thigh (one routine in the binary, copied per class): their pictures, the body's pieces drawn over them (and their layer), their cut lines (the binary's `_pelvisHitPoints`, `_kneeHitPoints`... tables, `GameData`) and the size of the box the cut-out piece is taken by (80×64; 256×140 for the knee and thigh).
- **Insertion** (`injuries/Insertion.cpp`): a pin to put in: the cutter opens its line, the pin then waits on the tongs' tray, and let go within 32 points of its place (73 points down the cut) it is pushed in. **LargeCutScar** (`injuries/Cuts.cpp`): a large cut's scar, stitched and burnt, with no "correct" or score.
- **Crab** (`injuries/Crab.cpp`), the Flash AS2's (the monsters, below): walks across the body in rows, nipping small cuts; the chainsaw cracks its shell (which flies off as a `SurfaceExtraction` piece to take out, and the crab runs faster), then kills it, bleeding blue (`BlueBloodSplatter`).
- **Mission 7's** (`Cuts.cpp`, `Extraction.cpp`, `Dove.cpp`): **ClownNose** (the red nose over a line to cut; it pops off in a burst of blood, leaving a cut to stitch, burn and cream; the clown's eyes follow the finger); **UnderSurfaceExtractionClown** (the endless scarf under the skin: cut open, then pulled 800 points with the tongs, slipping back in while let go; out comes a **Bunny**, an **Extraction**: a thing the tongs simply lift to the tray, "correct" when taken); **Dove** (the Flash AS2's, below: glides low across the body nipping cuts, waits off the side, crosses back high as a shadow dropping **Droplet**s that land as white **DoveSplatter** only the vacuum clears; the battery shocks it on a low pass, feathers flying (**FeatherSplatter**); three shocks kill it (a shock while it dies does nothing); shocking its shadow shocks the patient); **Burn** (a misused lighter's or battery's mark, creamed away with no score).
- **Mission 8's** (`Poison.cpp`, `Splatter.cpp`): **Poison** (green blobs on the liver, pulsing; the injection bursts each into three **PoisonSplatter**s, thirteen drops apiece that sap the heart — a third of the drops left (rounded down), every 250 frames (the iPad's one splatter table; the Flash AS2's typed splatters every 350) — until vacuumed. Poison's render never called InjuryGraphic's, so on the iPad a blob neither hurt nor spread, its spreading special being dead code; kept so); the knee and thigh transplants (above).
- **Mission 9's** (`Dove.cpp`), in the dark (the torch): **InsectBoss**, a wasp, the Flash AS2's (below). In flight it is out of reach and stings (a small cut where it is, kept on the body); resting it can be shocked with the battery. Each shock leaves a poison splatter and a poison blob; the third kills it (it oozes poison as it dies, then leaves a last blob and its body: the remains, below).

- **Shared helpers** (`InjuryGraphic`): `oneHitBox` (the one box of a small injury), `columnHitBoxes` (a cut's column, optionally offset), `lineHitBoxes` + `colourBox` (a dashed line from a binary table of (x, y, turn), coloured by the stage's line: the transplants, the big tick's, the skull's), `waitOnTray` (an insertion's piece on the tongs' tray: pins, plugs, the pill, the transistor, the skull's cap), `fadeOut`, `traceMoved`.
- **Panel** (`menus/Panel.cpp`) is the mission's view controller: the tutorial panel and popup, and the touches: on the HUD a tool card picks its tool (or puts it down) and the pause button pauses (`selectTool`, shared with the number keys); a finger with a tool works it at half the screen point (the world's points). The nib's own tool and pause buttons are hidden.
- **The HUD and pointer are the Flash AS2's** (`menus/Hud.cpp`), taken from the Flash original on the user's request: its `ui` and `cursor` symbols, from `re/flash/as2/.../media/assets.swf` extracted by the build into `port/games/as2/flash/` (`extract_swf`, as Christmas's package; the `as2_flash` target), drawn as vector art by `VectorArt` with the Flash stage (600×460) at the screen's height, centred. The tools are its numbered cards across the top (the number keys' order, 1–9 and 0), at the tool's level, dimmed while the mission does not offer it (as Flash's `Panel.init`), lit while in hand; the graveyard's cream and needle are the syrup and the phial, the vampire's injection the blood vial; a tutorial's arrow plays under its card (Flash's `effect<tool>`). The JogLog dial top left: its face's colour and runner by the heart (GameState's runner state: 1–4 by the heart, 5 the death, which plays once and lies still on its last frame: `manny_death`'s class stops it on 33, added with `VectorArt::addStop`), the BPM, the pause button in its corner; the port's own runner animations (the shock, the brain out) are the iPad pictures drawn in the runner's place at its size (measured on the running jogger's first frame: its bounds as it ran had the pictures jump about with its stride). The time bottom left with its drip running down with the mission's clock (a brain out's countdown instead while it runs), the score bottom right (Flash's thousands commas), Bleed's countdown (Flash's `bleedCountdown`). The pointer is Flash's cursor with the tool in hand (`cursor` frames by tool; the vampire's injection is `blood`, the graveyard's needle and cream `vial` and `syrup`), drawn as `platform::PointerArt`, so it is where the mouse is at each display frame, and so are what the finger holds (the tongs' catch, the injection's plunger: `SceneManager::onPointer`, named by the tool each pass); the system's pointer is hidden meanwhile and comes back over the pause menu and popups. **On a touch device** (the mobile ports to come) there is no pointer at all ([rules.md](rules.md) §7): neither this cursor nor the hand over Flash buttons is drawn, the finger being the pointer, and the torch is steered by the tilt; the PC build always draws them. The play area's top for new injuries is below the cards (world y 80; Flash's `minY`), as squid ink landed under a card could not be cleared. A press picks a card (or pauses) only on its art as last drawn: the tool's picture or its card (`<tool>highlight`, white, orange while held; Flash listened on the picture's clip alone, whose own card shape its level-3 frames lack) (`Hud::partAt`, with the look the HUD drew: each card held on its level's frame; Flash's MOUSE_DOWN on the clip; the tilted cards' bounding boxes reached below them, so a press just under the band took a tool, and tested without the look a card played through its frames, so a press on it now and then fell through to the patient). The number keys pick the tools as Flash's did (the same key puts it down). The injection is the Flash AS2's (`tools/Injection.cpp`, after its `Injection.as`): pressed on the patient (or an injury that wants it) its syringe (`injectionBar<level>`, one of its five mazes at random, the vampire's red; Flash's `random() * 1 + 1` only ever picked the first) appears with the needle at the finger, drawn at the stage's scale and turned away from the stage's edges (in the scene, layer 8, under a blackout's dark: only its torch lights it, as the rest; drawn over everything, it had stood out on the black); held, the finger goes up its path (`floor`) to the ring (`hit`): the shot given; a `wall` or off the path is a wrong use; letting go puts it away. The HUD's cursor gives way to the system's pointer meanwhile, as Flash's did. In debug mode it traces a way up the maze for the play-tests (`injection path x,y ...`, a breadth-first search over its shapes). The iPad's close-up, its channel tables and the plunger's trail are gone. The tools' trails are continuous along the stroke (`ITool::trail`): besides the original's puff every few passes where the finger is (kept for a still finger), the lighter lays a flame every 6 points of the finger's path since the last pass, the cream (and the graveyard's phial) a puff every 5 and a spray every third, the injection's plunger a trail dot every 5, each shown at the moment within the pass the finger passed there; the original's one puff a pass left a dotted trail behind a smooth cursor. A trail's pieces (`Game::addTrailEffect`, `addInjectionTrail`) last half their effect's life, fading out from the start (`ToolEffect::makeTrail`, the shape's `fade`; their frames as ever), so the stroke does not linger; the still finger's puffs keep their whole life. Pinch zoom needs two fingers: none on the PC.
- **The pause and death clipboards, the loading screen and the bandages are the Flash AS2's** too (taken on the user's request, the iPad's being zoomed-in, cut or plain pictures): the clips of its `shell.swf` and `shell_preloader.swf`, extracted by the build into `port/games/as2/flash-shell/` and `flash-preloader/`, drawn on the same Flash stage (`menus/FlashStage.cpp`: each package's art, the stage's place on the screen, Flash's score commas and clock). Their big pieces took up to 40 ms a frame to rasterise as each frame came, so the screens that can start them have them made in the background as they open, and again every 20 s while they stay (`bandages::prefetch`). Used where Flash used it: quitting a mission from the pause or the obituary.

## Testing

`port/tools/surgeon2_playtest.py` plays the game as a player would: it starts the exe hidden with a scratch save and drives it live (`AS_CONTROL`), reading the trace (`-d`): screens shown, `texture <gl id> <name>` as each picture loads (to read an `AS_FILM` log by name), `stage <n>`, tutorials, `tools <offered>`, for each injury `injury <event> <class> ... tool <tool> ... at x,y rot r hidden 0|1` with its boxes (`  box x,y health`, screen coordinates; health 0 once treated, which the tongs still take an object by), `fade done` (a new stage's art has faded in: the tools work again), each injury again when a tool is let go after wearing its boxes down (`injury worked ...`, with its boxes' health now), the drops (`drop landed|gone blood|splatter x,y`: `splatter` for a splatter that is itself one of the stage's injuries, which holds the stage up until vacuumed, `blood` for the rest; `blood cleared` with a new background), the heart rate (`heart <n>`, each 5 of change), what waits on the tongs' tray (`tray <class> at x,y to x,y`, `tray empty`) and the injection's syringe when it opens (`injection shown for ...`, then `injection path x,y ...`, a way up its maze), and where an injury that walks about is (`injury moved #<serial> at x,y open 0|1`, every 12 points; open: within reach), each drop by its number (`drop landed|gone ... #n`), a text-message alert's button (`alert <title> button x,y`), the injection's close-up shutting (`injection closed`), each sound effect as it starts (`sound <key>`), the shake (`shake on|off`) and a gauge to hold (`injury balance #n gauge g needle n dir d axis across|down`), and on a Flash screen (the pause, the obituary, the discharge form), once its clips rest, where each piece that takes clicks is (`flash piece <path> at x,y`; the driver presses the discharge form's continue there). An injury's tool can also be `wait` (nothing to do now), `tap` (a button on it), `rub` (rubbed fast), `shake` (the pointer shaken) or `anything`. The driver sends `hover x y` (the pointer moved, not pressed) to shake, `text <chars>` for keys pressed, and taps the arrow keys (`key <frame>:left|right|up|down`, held for that frame) every other frame while a gauge is shown, whatever else it is doing. A run that tries the very same thing 20 times to no avail stops as stuck, its trace kept (`trace.txt` beside the save; `--keep` keeps the save and its trace of a passing run too). Each injury's line ends with its serial (`#n`), which the driver keys it by (its screen place moves when the screen shakes). Each stage is begun once its art has faded in, and left as soon as the trace says the next has begun. It taps through the title screen, the intro and the cut scene, answers the tutorials, picks the tool each injury wants (only once the HUD offers it) with its number key, as a PC player does, keeps every press off the HUD (the card band across the top, the time and score cards at the bottom: a press there picks a tool or pauses; a stroke starts off it and may cross it, as a line passing under the cards must), and uses it as a player would: along the boxes (lighter, cream, cutter), zigzag across the cut (stitch), along a transplant's line box by box, each the nearest not yet passed (chainsaw: its table's order jumps across the piece), the injection's close-up opened over an injury that wants it (a poison blob), pull out along the object's direction and on to the tray (tongs; hand over hand, let go and taken again where first pressed, while it has not come out: the clown's scarf is longer than the play area); a hidden injury is first found with the scanner (swept onto it); once the vacuum is offered the blood is sucked up when it gathers (and at once the first time, as its tutorial asks); what waits on the tray is taken to where it goes; an injury that walks about is chased, the tool held on it (`down`, then a `move` each frame, `up`), and one that flies is shocked with a battery tap a little ahead of it when it is within reach (before anything else; while it is away the rest is seen to, and from 60 frames before its shortest rest off the body so far would end — the dove's low pass, the wasp's landing, both brief — the driver waits for it with the battery rather than start something long); a shocked patient (the heart at 0) is revived with the battery; splatter only the vacuum clears is vacuumed as soon as it lands (a poison blob's saps the heart while it lies); a stage that asks for an injury class the port lacks fails the run; a walker is waited for a little ahead on its way, the tool held still until it has gone past (the Flash crab shies from the pointer but cannot turn before it has slowed); a saw line whose boxes all lie under the tool cards is begun just below them and dragged up; the heart is given a shot whenever it is under 30 (not again for 10 seconds if a shot did not help it), and topped up to 55 before the big centipede's eye is injected (its craze bites hard); once the injection is offered it is given at once and whenever the heart falls under 45 (one shot a round, up to four under 15) (pressing on the patient or the injury, then drawing the finger along the traced path up the maze to the ring); the monsters' remains are lifted to the tray with the tongs, as any lifted thing. Scenario `mission1` plays the first mission to the win (4 stages, every tutorial, score ~10 700 with time to spare); `missionN` plays missions 1 to N in turn from a new game, each picked on the level select after the last win (mission 2: 9 stages, the scanner's and vacuum's tutorials); with `--save <dir>` (a kept run's save, so the earlier missions were won by real play) `fromN` goes through the main menu and file select to the level select and plays missions N on, `onlyN` mission N alone (21–30 through the file select's CFO folder; 10 and 20 need A grades on the missions before, so a seeded save); `start` only enters mission 1. For playing by hand while tests and builds run, `port/build-play/` holds a copy of the exe (it finds the same `port/games/as2`). Run them under the ASan build too ([port.md](port.md#building-and-running)).
